What I'm building now

Felix

A QUIC-based data broker in Rust: low-latency pub/sub, a key-value cache, and a durable event log with resumable subscriptions.

Built for high fanout, predictable tail latency, and strict slow-consumer isolation — one subscriber having a bad day should not become everybody's problem. A framed binary protocol over QUIC streams unifies event streaming and request/response caching, with explicit control over multiplexing, batching, and flow control. Single-node today; multi-node clustering in progress.

How I work

Three decisions I'd defend

Taken from Felix, where I get to make all of them. Open one.

The broker enqueues publish jobs and fans out over a shared-frame path with dense stream handles on the hot path, so per-subscriber cost stays close to a write. Each subscription gets its own unidirectional delivery stream, and byte-budgeted admission control at both client and broker ingest keeps a slow consumer from backing up into everyone else.

Clients hold a connection pool of long-lived stream workers; cache requests carry a request ID and multiplex over them, and the broker answers on the same stream from a single read loop. Put and get with TTL, log-backed and durable when the broker has a storage directory — the same protocol that carries events, so there is one envelope to reason about.

A standalone runner validates that framing and binary encoding match the shared test vectors. Anything that passes can interoperate without relying on Rust-specific behavior — which is also why the wire protocol, client SDK and transport are Apache-2.0 while the server components are source-available under ELv2.
